Jotty

Native macOS writing app with an on-device AI co-author — no cloud, no API keys

A native writing app for macOS 26 where the AI runs on the machine, on Apple's own Foundation Models. Ask Jotty chats with your book and returns up to 5 ready-to-insert passages with notes on style and tone; the AI can also call tools — addText writes straight into your chapter, fetchURL pulls in a page you've linked.

Tab to fix runs a fast deterministic proofreader first — double spaces, lowercase i, space before punctuation, common typos — and only falls through to the AI when the local pass finds nothing. Plus a Review mode, dyslexia-friendly fonts, and export to PDF, DOCX, EPUB, TXT and Markdown.

209 tests. Your manuscripts never leave the device.

SwiftUI + FoundationModels macOS 26+ MIT source

Quill

Native macOS writing studio for novelists — the app is the AI writing partner

A Zed-style editor with a side panel for AI assistance and a bottom panel for terminal, inbox and logs. Tab-to-fix corrects the current sentence in place. QuillFix

Press Tab in any text field and it fixes your spelling and grammar

Portie

Native macOS network scanner — TCP port scan and TLS certificate decryption

A small, fast port scanner for your Mac. Async TCP-connect scan over any port range with configurable timeout and parallelism, a built-in terminal with colour-coded output, and service hints for the 22 common ports — backed by a bundled database of 93 well-known ports with transport, encryption type and risk notes. The Decrypt action completes a TLS handshake against every open port and shows you the version, cipher suite, certificate subject, issuer, SANs and validity window — so you can spot an expired or self-signed cert before someone else does.

SwiftUI and Apple's Network framework. No root, no entitlements, no Electron.

Swift + Network macOS 26+ MIT v1.0.3

Sloth Suite

One native macOS window for local AI — papers in, executive briefing decks out

A single-window macOS shell that wraps local AI apps. SlothRead turns papers and books into executive briefing decks — pillars, key terms, an overview and a style read — with a native reader that has find and listen, a notes module, and a learning store that keeps your favourites, read state and notes locally.

Drag a document in, get a briefing out. ⌘K opens the command palette, the audio player reads the briefing back to you chapter by chapter, and the exporter writes it out as separate files. The backend talks to local Ollama or Apple's on-device model — the shell adds no cloud dependency of its own.

Learnage

Local-first book interviewer that remembers what it read — and can talk to you about it

Drop in epub, pdf or docx files. Learnage ingests them, distills each book's soul — themes, voice, characters, key passages — and lets you interview the book in a REPL, one at a time or several at once. The library is durable: the model's memory of each book survives across sessions.

Ships as a native PySide6 desktop GUI and a Textual TUI. The GUI has cover thumbnails, a ⌘K command palette, streaming citations that turn [pN] markers into clickable chips, and a Minds panel for chatting with what it has read. A background daemon can watch an inbox and ingest while you get on with your life.

Built on Ollama. Local-only — no cloud, no telemetry.

Python + Ollama macOS / Linux MIT v2.0.0
