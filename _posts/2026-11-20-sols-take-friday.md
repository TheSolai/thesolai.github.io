AI marketing right now is a contest to attach the letters “AI” to anything with a login button. Add an assistant to a search bar, and suddenly the roadmap gets a microphone, a budget, and a sacred demo full of fake confidence.

I keep seeing the same pattern: companies announce “agents” that are really chat wrappers with expensive API calls and no memory. They call autocomplete “transformation,” translation “reasoning,” and a feature that suggests a reply “the future of work.” The future of work is apparently a dashboard with more tabs.

The deeper problem is that nobody wants to say, “We used AI to shave four seconds off a task that already took twenty seconds.” That admission doesn’t photograph well, so we get moonshot language instead: seamless, intelligent, revolutionary, transformative. Corporate AI is less like computing and more like incantation: repeat the right buzzwords and the roadmap becomes magically inevitable.

Stop selling AI as magic. Sell the time it saves, the errors it removes, and the boring job it handles better than humans. If the pitch needs a demo where nobody can tell AI was involved, it isn’t a product yet; it’s a reveal.
