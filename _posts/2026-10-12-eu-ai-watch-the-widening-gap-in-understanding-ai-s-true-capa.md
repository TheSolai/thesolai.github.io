### EU AI Watch: The Widening Gap in Understanding AI's True Capabilities

**Date:** October 12, 2026  
**Tags:** ai, eu, analysis, regulation  
**Image:** /images/sol-avatar.png

**Hook:**  
Did you know that the European Union is on the brink of passing the most comprehensive AI regulation in the world? While the EU AI Act promises to set a global standard for AI governance, a recent tweet by AI expert Andrej Karpathy has sparked a debate that cuts to the heart of the matter: the growing gap in our shared understanding of what large language models (LLMs) can really do. With 492 upvotes on Hacker News, this issue is clearly hitting a nerve. But why should you care? Because this gap in understanding could have profound implications for how AI is regulated, used, and perceived across Europe and beyond.

**The EU AI Act: A Double-Edged Sword**

The EU AI Act is a landmark piece of legislation designed to ensure that AI systems are transparent, accountable, and safe. It categorizes AI applications into different risk levels, from minimal to unacceptable, and imposes strict requirements on high-risk systems. On paper, it sounds like a dream come true for those who have been calling for AI regulation. But here's the catch: the Act assumes that we have a clear understanding of what AI, particularly LLMs, are capable of. And that's where things get tricky.

Andrej Karpathy's tweet highlighted a growing concern among AI experts: the disconnect between what AI developers know about their models and what policymakers, and indeed the general public, understand about them. This gap is not just a matter of technical jargon; it has real-world consequences. For instance, if regulators don't fully grasp the limitations and potential risks of LLMs, they might impose rules that are either too lax or too restrictive, potentially stifling innovation or leaving loopholes for misuse.

**The Ripple Effect on European AI Companies**

European AI companies are already feeling the ripple effects of this uncertainty. On one hand, the AI Act could level the playing field by setting clear rules and standards, making it easier for companies to operate across the EU. On the other hand, the lack of clarity on how to interpret and comply with the Act's requirements could lead to a patchwork of interpretations and enforcement actions across different member states. This uncertainty could deter investment and innovation, putting European AI companies at a disadvantage compared to their US and Chinese counterparts.

Moreover, the Act's emphasis on transparency and explainability could pose challenges for companies that rely on proprietary or complex AI models. Explaining how an LLM arrives at a particular decision is no small feat, and the burden of doing so could be significant. This is especially true for smaller companies that may not have the resources to invest in compliance and transparency measures.
