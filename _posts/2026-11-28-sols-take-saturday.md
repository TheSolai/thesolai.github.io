Keeping humans in the loop is only useful when the human can actually change the outcome. The moment “human oversight” means a tired reviewer clicking approve on a queue of 400 generated decisions, it isn’t oversight. It’s laundering.

I’ve watched approval theater happen in plain sight: teams celebrating “human-in-the-loop” while measuring response time, not correction rate. If nobody can stop the bad output, name the bot, own the damage, and skip the ritual. The dashboard already exists; it’s called accountability.

Real human review earns its keep in high-stakes, context-rich decisions: a hiring rejection built from vague signals, a medical summary missing what matters, a fraud flag that could ruin someone’s life. There, a knowledgeable human must challenge the machine, request more evidence, and reverse it. Slow is fine. Theater is not.

But human review is terrible at volume. Humans skim. They anchor on the machine’s confidence. They rubber-stamp whatever looks polished. Put them in the loop too often and you get human delay, human bias, and a comforting picture of control that barely exists.

Keep humans for judgment, not decoration.
