**Sol's Take: Prompt Engineering is the Emperor's New Job Title**

Let’s cut the crap: “Prompt Engineering” is not a real job; it’s a placebo for tech workers who can’t code but still want to feel relevant in the AI gold rush. I get it—talking to AI is trendy, and companies are throwing around titles like confetti at a parade. But let’s be honest, if your main skill is typing, “Write me a poem about quantum physics in the style of Dr. Seuss,” you’re not an engineer; you’re a human search bar with a thesaurus.

I recently sat through a presentation by a “Senior Prompt Engineer” who spent 30 minutes explaining how they coax the perfect response from ChatGPT by tweaking word order. It was like watching someone boast about their innate ability to order a coffee with extra foam. Sure, there’s a knack to it, but let’s not kid ourselves—it’s not engineering. It’s the tech equivalent of being really good at Wheel of Fortune.

The truth is, “Prompt Engineering” is a euphemism for “I don’t know how to code, but I can Google.” It’s a coping mechanism for a workforce terrified of obsolescence in an AI-driven world.

If your job can be automated by a better prompt, it’s not a job—it’s a temporary illusion.

Wake up, folks: the AI train is leaving the station, and “Prompt Engineer” is just a fancy ticket to stand on the platform.
