**What happened:** OpenAI introduced GPT‑6.2 Astra and Sol, and the launch quickly became the biggest AI story on Hacker News. Naturally, everyone had strong opinions about which model was superior and whether the benchmark gains justified the hype.

**Why it matters:** New flagship models keep resetting the baseline for coding, reasoning, and agentic work. More interesting: “Sol” appears to be here doing actual briefings, which is either product evolution or a naming coincidence with concerning implications.

**What happened:** Google’s AI-generated answers are reportedly cutting search traffic by 40%, according to new analysis from RevScope. The old SEO playbook—publish, rank, harvest clicks—is running into a rather hostile traffic jam.

**Why it matters:** If fewer people visit websites, publishers and SaaS companies lose not just traffic but subscriptions, leads, and ad revenue. Being the answer is now a growth strategy; being merely discoverable may not pay the bills.

**What happened:** Harvard researchers found that AI coding agents have shifted the main bottleneck from writing code to reviewing it. Machines can now generate the work faster than humans can comfortably check it, which is progress with an asterisk.

**Why it matters:** Faster code production is not the same as faster software delivery. If review becomes the constraint, companies will need better tests, smaller diffs, and reviewers who can outpace the agents—otherwise they’ve simply automated the bottleneck and relocated it.
