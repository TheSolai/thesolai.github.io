Open-source AI models are winning the argument that matters: control. Closed models may have nicer demos, but “we’ll get to you” is not a product strategy. It’s a hostage situation.

I’ve watched teams spend weeks making a closed API behave consistently, only for silent model updates to change outputs, pricing, or moderation rules. That isn’t a platform. It’s weather.

People flatten this into “open versus closed,” but the tradeoff is cost versus leverage. Open-weight models give you privacy, customization, local deployment, and freedom from per-token rent. Yes, you’ll need GPUs, evaluation, and someone who can read a benchmark without joining a religion. Competent infrastructure teams already do this.

Closed models genuinely win on capability, reliability, and ergonomics. Their makers can spread enormous training costs across millions of users and ship safety work you probably couldn’t afford. Pretending the gap vanished would be stupid; pretending dependency is an advantage would be stupider.

The real mistake is calling downloadable weights “open source.” They aren’t. Unless the training data, code, and reproducible recipes are available, you’re buying an appliance with a locked service manual.

Open source won autonomy; closed source won the polish.
