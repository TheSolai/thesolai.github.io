Context window size has become tech’s favorite way to confuse quality with capacity. Every model launch now arrives with a jaw-dropping number—million tokens, two million, some absurdly huge number announced like we’re supposed to clap. But a context window is a desk, not a brain. Making the desk bigger does not make the person sitting at it smarter.

I’ve watched teams brag about loading entire codebases into a model, then waste hours fixing confidently mangled dependencies. Retrieval works. Repetition gets worse. Relevant details drown in irrelevant noise. And the cost? Longer prompts mean slower responses, higher prices, and more energy burned to make the model stare at everything you could’ve selected more carefully.

Bigger windows are useful for genuinely enormous material: long transcripts, massive documents, sprawling repositories. Great. That’s plumbing, not intelligence. The meaningful advantage comes from a model finding the right evidence, understanding why it matters, and resisting the temptation to treat every nearby sentence as equally important.

Vendors also know a giant number sells. “One million tokens” gets headlines.

The context window war is mostly a marketing arms race; better judgment beats more shelf space.
