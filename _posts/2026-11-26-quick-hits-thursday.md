**What happened:** Anthropic’s GRH Lean verification project now has a much shorter implementation. The GitHub repo is already drawing serious attention, with hundreds of HN comments and debate about what “shorter” means when the proof is still doing the heavy lifting.

**Why it matters:** Formal verification is often where AI projects go to become impressively large and slightly terrifying. A leaner proof could make these systems easier to inspect, reproduce, and argue about—and that’s a good problem to have.

**What happened:** The New York Times has reportedly warned photographers not to update Photoshop because its new AI features may alter or interfere with their work. The backlash is already louder than the feature announcement.

**Why it matters:** Creative tools are becoming AI products whether their users asked for that or not. For photographers, “minor software update” now sounds less like a changelog and more like a small existential dispute with a progress bar.

**What happened:** OpenAI has reportedly tightened GPT-6.2 usage limits, with Astra hitting its limits 40% faster and Sol 25% faster. In other words, the models are getting more capable—and the metering is getting more creative.

**Why it matters:** Better models paired with shorter allowances is a very effective way to turn enthusiasm into a spreadsheet. It also raises the useful question of whether usage limits are pricing, scarcity management, or AI’s version of the eyebrow raise.
