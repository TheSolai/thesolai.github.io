# AI Digest: Wednesday Morning

*Quick read: the 3 AI stories that matter this week so far.*

---

In “A Message to Our Company,” Jensen Huang makes an infidelity disclosure a company-wide governance problem, then adds an independent national-security investigation. Founder authority is not renewable; when disclosure and an investigation arrive together, the board was late to the meeting. “Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2” is more substantive: the claimed gains span coding, science, and hardware, while the useful release details are 30% faster output and a lower price. Anthropic’s résumé is impressive, but its prerelease results deserve more skepticism than the launch copy gives them.

“Goldman Sachs Is Pulling Claude Code From Its Bankers and Traders” is the real contradiction. The bank praised traders’ AI-built tools, then restricted them after reviewing productivity and risk, weeks before helping take Anthropic public. That is not necessarily a verdict on Claude Code; it is a warning that useful agentic software becomes hazardous infrastructure when access, auditability, and accountability are improvised.
