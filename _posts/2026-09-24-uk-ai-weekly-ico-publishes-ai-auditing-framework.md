# UK ICO publishes AI auditing framework

The UK's Information Commissioner's Office released a 90-page AI auditing framework this week, giving UK organisations a concrete checklist for evaluating their AI systems against GDPR principles.

The framework covers five core areas: data minimisation, transparency, accuracy, human oversight, and accountability. Each area includes a maturity model with four levels and a self-assessment questionnaire.

The ICO's tone is pragmatic. The document acknowledges that perfect compliance is unrealistic and instead focuses on "demonstrable compliance" — organisations that can show their reasoning and evidence for AI decisions will be treated more leniently than those who can't.

The framework is voluntary, but ICO enforcement powers remain unchanged. Organisations that ignore the framework and end up in breach of GDPR will face the same penalties as before.

**Practical takeaways:**

1. **Document your data minimisation decisions.** The ICO expects to see evidence of why you collected the data you did, why you kept it as long as you did, and why you processed it the way you did.

2. **Transparency is more than a privacy notice.** The framework makes clear that users should know when they're interacting with an AI system and what the AI system is doing with their data. A link to a privacy policy buried three clicks deep doesn't cut it.

3. **Human oversight needs to be meaningful.** A "human in the loop" who rubber-stamps every AI decision isn't meaningful oversight. The framework expects human reviewers to have authority, time, and information to challenge AI outputs.

4. **Accuracy applies to AI outputs, not just training data.** The ICO holds organisations responsible for the outputs of their AI systems, including hallucinations and confident-but-wrong answers.

5. **Accountability is structural.** The framework asks for clear ownership of AI risks at the executive level. "The data team owns it" isn't sufficient.

UK organisations should download the framework, do the self-assessment, identify their gaps, and start closing them. The framework is a gift — it's the first time the ICO has spelled out what "good" looks like. The bar isn't impossibly high, but it's also not nothing.

The European context: the EU AI Act enters its next enforcement phase in November. The UK is not bound by the EU AI Act, but UK organisations operating in the EU will be. The ICO's framework is positioned as a UK-aligned approach that doesn't conflict with the AI Act, which is the right posture for cross-border operations.
