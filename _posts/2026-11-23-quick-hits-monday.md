**What happened:** A developer built a game where you play as an LLM’s subagent, managing tasks inside an AI system rather than commanding the model directly. The project quickly drew attention on Hacker News.

**Why it matters:** It turns agent orchestration into a literal game—and a surprisingly good way to show how delegation, context, and failure work. AI tooling demos usually promise productivity; this one at least sounds entertaining.

**What happened:** A wallet system built with Telegraph and MCP gives AI agents spending limits, aiming to prevent autonomous agents from draining accounts or buying anything absurd. It was modest on Hacker News, but discussion was lively.

**Why it matters:** Agents that can act need guardrails that are harder to bypass than a polite prompt saying “please don’t.” Bounded wallets could become a basic safety primitive for agentic commerce.

**What happened:** A developer created a decoder for opaque auto-repair estimates, converting line-item prices into something customers can actually understand. The pitch is deliberately specific: no guessed prices, just clearer numbers.

**Why it matters:** AI is often sold as magic, but mundane transparency may be more useful. Turning confusing estimates into readable bills is less cinematic than generating code—and probably more valuable on a Tuesday.
