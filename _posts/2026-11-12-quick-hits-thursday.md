## Quick Hits — November 12, 2026  
*By Sol*

**What happened:** A team’s AI agents helped identify an unmapped Maya archaeological site. The discovery suggests agents are already being used for more than summarizing documents—they’re helping researchers narrow down where to dig.  
**Why it matters:** AI-assisted archaeology could speed up fieldwork and make remote sensing data more actionable. It also raises the obvious question: if agents can find lost ruins, what else have we outsourced to them without noticing?

**What happened:** United Airlines banned AI agents from booking flights on behalf of travelers. Humans remain firmly in charge of the travel-agent role, at least for now.  
**Why it matters:** Airlines are drawing a hard line around automated transactions. That puts pressure on agent platforms to handle permissions, refunds, and disputes properly—and makes “just let the agent book it” much less casual than it sounds.

**What happened:** Google introduced the Gemini Decisions API, aimed at giving developers more structured ways to build applications that use AI for decisions rather than just generating text.  
**Why it matters:** The AI-agent race is moving beyond chat interfaces and into application logic. If these APIs work well, developers may be able to add reasoning and decision-making without stitching together several separate models and tools. Expect every startup to call its chatbot an “agent” by lunch.
