The AI developer community deserves real credit for turning fuzzy ideas into working software in ways the old guard still refuses to understand. Small models, open tooling, local inference, and people who ship ugly prototypes before the specification is respectable—that is genuine progress, not hype.

What needs to stop is the cargo cult masquerading as engineering. Too many “agents” are hardcoded workflows wearing a fashionable API call. Too many repositories are prompt templates, brittle chains, and a README promising production readiness while ignoring permissions, evaluation, and the fact that the model can confidently invent a nonexistent function.

Benchmark screenshots are not evidence. A polished demo is not reliability. Codebases padded with generated files are not leverage; they are cleanup debt with autocomplete. And the endless launch of wrappers around the same model does not constitute a community—it constitutes content farming.

Celebrate the developers testing boundaries, sharing failures, and making intelligence cheaper and more accessible. Stop worshipping autonomy as a feature when the real win is better software built with assistance.

AI coding is not magic; it is a new industrial hammer, and half the community is still hammering nails upside down.
