MS Paint, the humble graphics editor that’s been around since the dawn of Windows, now invisibly watermarks even locally generated images with a Globally Unique Identifier (GUID). And this isn't just a quirky update—it's a sign of the times, a reflection of the growing tension between innovation and privacy in the AI landscape.

The news, which recently scored a solid 646 points on Hacker News, has sparked a flurry of discussions and debates. This watermark is invisible to the naked eye but can be detected through a simple reverse engineering process.

So, why is this a big deal? Well, for starters, it raises questions about user privacy and consent. While watermarking can be a useful tool for tracking the origin of images, especially in the age of AI-generated content, it also means that every doodle, every edited photo, is now linked to your machine. This has implications for anonymity and the freedom to create without fear of surveillance.

Moreover, this development highlights the increasing role of AI in everyday software. It reflects a broader trend of integrating AI into tools we use daily, often without our knowledge or explicit consent.

What this means is that the line between traditional software and AI-powered applications is becoming increasingly blurred.
