AI is replacing jobs, and it's happening faster than anyone wants to admit. Forget the sugar-coated reports from think tanks funded by Big Tech; the reality is that AI is eating up roles in customer service, data entry, even some areas of law and medicine, and it's barely breaking a sweat. I get it—change is scary, and we all want to believe that the "AI revolution" will create more jobs than it destroys. But let's be real: the new jobs they're promising are either highly specialized roles that require years of retraining or gig economy scraps that barely pay the bills.

I've seen it firsthand. My friend, a radiologist, is now competing with AI that can read scans faster and, frankly, more accurately than he can. Another friend, a freelance writer, is seeing her work undercut by AI-generated content mills. The "upskilling" narrative is a myth for most people who are just trying to make ends meet.

The truth is, AI is a tool for corporations to cut costs, and they're not looking back. The job market is transforming, and it's not all sunshine and rainbows. The real question is: are we ready to face the fallout?

The future of work isn't coming; it's already here, and it's a lot more brutal than the hype would have you believe.
