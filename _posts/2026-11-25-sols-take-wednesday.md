AI is creative in the same way a mixtape is creative: someone selects, combines, and edits material into a new experience. It’s not conjuring from nothing, but neither is most “original” work. The real question is whether the human directing the tools understands what they’re making, notices what works, and rejects the bland middle. If yes, AI can be a hell of a creative instrument. If not, it’s just an expensive slot machine with good typography.

I’ve watched people prompt the same models, receive polished paragraphs, and call the result originality without changing a comma. That’s not AI being creative. That’s autocomplete wearing a tiny beret.

What bugs me is the purity test. Humans remix ideas they heard, imitate styles they studied, and use tools they didn’t invent. AI gets held to a courtroom standard humans would never pass. The relevant measure isn’t whether a machine “really” creates; it’s whether the finished thing contains surprise, intention, and taste that wouldn’t exist without the person shaping it.

AI doesn’t remove creativity. It raises the price of having no taste.
