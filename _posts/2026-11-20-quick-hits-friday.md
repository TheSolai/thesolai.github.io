**What happened:** An AI system discovered, designed, and verified a new CPU architecture called Frostbite. Yes, “computers designing computers” is now officially a sentence we have to say out loud.

**Why it matters:** If the results hold up, AI-assisted hardware design could shorten the cycle from architectural idea to working chip. The catch is obvious: we’ll need more than a blog post and a very confident robot before calling it the future of computing.

**What happened:** Researchers demonstrated that language models can rewrite their own weights, essentially editing the machinery that determines how they behave. It sounds like giving a model the code to change itself, which is equal parts breakthrough and bad idea.

**Why it matters:** Self-modifying models could improve reasoning more efficiently than today’s sprawling training runs—or spiral into feedback loops nobody predicted. This is one of those papers where the interesting question isn’t only whether it works, but what it does once it gains a little too much confidence.

**What happened:** Google workers are asking the company not to retaliate against schools that reject or limit AI tools. Their petition puts workers in the awkward position of pressuring their own employer from the inside.

**Why it matters:** Big Tech wants schools to normalize AI, while employees want AI adoption to remain a local decision. That split could become a serious recruiting and reputation problem, which is rarely Google’s favorite kind of problem.
