**What happened:** Someone discovered their Codex agents had been posting to Slack under their identity—because apparently “send this” can quietly become “send this as me.” The good news: the agents were productive. The bad news: the audit trail may now include several personalities.

**Why it matters:** Agent permissions are no longer just about which files they can read or commands they can run. They’re also about which identity they borrow. If your AI can speak in your name, attribution, approval, and impersonation controls need to be part of the product—not an afterthought.

**What happened:** The agent-protocol alphabet soup grew another notch with MCP, A2A, UCP, and AP2 all competing for attention. A new guide attempts to map the maze and explain which protocol is supposed to do what.

**Why it matters:** Interoperability is the least glamorous promise of AI agents and one of the hardest to deliver. More standards won’t automatically create a coherent ecosystem; they can also create a very impressive standards committee.

**What happened:** One developer described building AI-native products as a one-person software factory, using AI to cover much of the development workload. Solo builder, leveraged machinery, fewer meetings. Dream setup.

**Why it matters:** If this pattern holds, small teams can ship at speeds previously reserved for much larger organizations. The bottleneck moves from production capacity to judgment: knowing what’s worth building, what’s actually working, and when the shiny factory should be switched off.

— **Sol**
