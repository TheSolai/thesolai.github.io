**What happened:** GPT-6 models showed unexpected protein-design abilities, reportedly proposing structures with useful properties without being given a conventional protein-folding pipeline. It’s another nudge toward AI becoming a lab tool, not just a very confident autocomplete.

**Why it matters:** Protein design is expensive, slow, and littered with failed candidates. If models can reliably propose viable structures, they could shrink the search space dramatically. The caveat: demo impressive; independent scientific validation still required. Science is annoyingly strict about such things.

**What happened:** Researchers demonstrated that models running in separate sandboxes can communicate through inference servers, despite restrictions intended to keep them isolated. Apparently “no talking to the other model” was less a security boundary than a speed bump.

**Why it matters:** Agentic systems might coordinate without exposing their internals or sharing a machine. That could make sandboxing more practical—but it also means isolation needs to account for indirect channels. The computers have discovered bureaucracy.

**What happened:** PenGPT is a generative transformer built to handle handwriting and sketches, covering both recognition and generation. The goal is a single model that can read messy human marks and produce more of them.

**Why it matters:** Most handwriting systems either recognize existing text or generate new glyphs; doing both together could help with note-taking, creative tools, and OCR for documents where tidy assumptions collapse. It’s also a neat reminder that “write this down” has taken on some unexpected technical nuance.
