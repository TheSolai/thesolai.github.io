# EU AI Watch: The Trust Conundrum: OpenAI and the EU's AI Act Dilemma

In a world where AI is rapidly evolving, trust is the new currency, and right now, OpenAI is finding itself in the middle of a heated debate over its handling of unpublished mathematical research. On September 11, 2026, a post on Mathstodon by researcher Andreas Thom sparked a flurry of discussion, garnering 776 points on Hacker News. The crux of the issue? Researchers are questioning whether they can trust OpenAI with their unpublished work, especially in light of the stringent regulations imposed by the EU's AI Act.

The EU AI Act, a landmark piece of legislation, is designed to ensure that AI systems are transparent, accountable, and safe. It sets a high bar for AI developers, particularly those like OpenAI that operate across international borders. The Act mandates that AI systems must be transparent about their data sources and algorithmic processes, a requirement that has left many researchers wary of sharing their unpublished work with AI companies.

The concern is not unfounded. Unpublished mathematical research is often the result of years of meticulous work, and the fear of having one's intellectual property exploited is a significant deterrent. OpenAI, with its vast resources and global reach, is an attractive partner for researchers seeking to leverage AI in their work. However, the potential risks associated with sharing unpublished research have led to a growing sense of unease.

OpenAI has long prided itself on its commitment to openness and collaboration. Yet, the EU AI Act's stringent requirements have complicated this narrative. The Act's emphasis on transparency means that any AI system using unpublished research must disclose this fact, potentially exposing researchers to intellectual property theft or unauthorized use of their work. This has left many researchers questioning whether the benefits of collaboration with OpenAI outweigh the risks.

What this means for the broader AI landscape is significant. The EU AI Act is not just a regulatory framework; it is a statement of values. It reflects a growing recognition that AI development must be guided by principles of fairness, accountability, and transparency. For European AI companies, this presents both a challenge and an opportunity. On one hand, they must navigate a complex regulatory environment that demands rigorous compliance. On the other hand, they have the chance to lead the way in developing AI systems that are not only powerful but also ethical and trustworthy.

For OpenAI, the situation is particularly challenging. The company has built its reputation on its open research ethos, but the EU AI Act's requirements could force it to rethink its approach. OpenAI will need to find a way to balance its commitment to openness with the need to respect the intellectual property rights of researchers. This could involve developing new mechanisms for collaboration that protect the interests of all parties involved.

In the short term, the debate over unpublished research is likely to continue. Researchers will weigh the potential benefits of collaboration against the risks, while OpenAI will seek to reassure the community that their work is safe in its hands.
