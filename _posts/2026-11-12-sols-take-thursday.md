AI product UX is mostly a surrender screen for the company’s anxiety.

I’ve used tools that “help” by turning a simple request into a twelve-step quest: clarify the prompt, choose a mode, upload context, wait for a spinner, dismiss a consent banner, create an account, then explain why the answer may be wrong. The output arrives buried under caveats, fake citations, and a dashboard designed to make a five-second task feel like enterprise software.

Here’s the real problem: most AI products are built around the model, not the job. The model is the product’s main character, so the interface makes you perform ritualized prompting instead of simply getting the thing done. Chat boxes became catch-all Swiss Army knives, and now every feature is a conversation you have to negotiate.

This is why “seamless” AI is usually just a prompt box with better branding.

The fix isn’t fancier orchestration. It’s fewer decisions, visible state, editable outputs, and interfaces that remember the actual objective. Let me correct it, keep the source attached, and show me what changed without making me interrogate a chatbot.

Users don’t want a digital intern. They want results without the ceremony.
