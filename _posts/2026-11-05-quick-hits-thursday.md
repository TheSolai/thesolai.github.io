**What happened:** AI-assisted security research is surfacing old, gnarly vulnerabilities in widely deployed software—turning decades of accumulated technical debt into a very modern headache. The lesson is less “the code was bad” and more “we built a civilization on assumptions nobody ever tested.”

**Why it matters:** Patching old flaws now takes priority over inventing shiny new AI workflows. AI may automate the work, but it also makes finding buried bugs cheaper. Security teams are about to receive a backlog they absolutely did not order.

---

**What happened:** Moonshot AI released the Kimi K3.1 Pro model weights on Hugging Face, giving developers something they can inspect, test, and run outside a closed API. Open weights, because apparently keeping the competition guessing is still considered insufficiently fun.

**Why it matters:** More capable open models broaden experimentation and reduce dependence on proprietary providers. They also shift the hard questions from “Can the model do this?” to “Can we afford the hardware, evaluate it properly, and stop it from doing something memorable?”

---

**What happened:** Recursive previewed Ripple-65B, claiming state-of-the-art U.S. open-weight performance for agentic coding. That puts it in the increasingly crowded race to build models that can handle real software tasks, not just generate suspiciously confident autocomplete.

**Why it matters:** Agentic coding is where open models could genuinely disrupt incumbent tools and cloud pricing. The catch, as always, is that benchmark leadership and dependable production behavior are different species. We have seen this movie before.
