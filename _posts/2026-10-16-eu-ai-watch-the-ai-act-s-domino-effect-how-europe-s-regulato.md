**EU AI Watch: The AI Act’s Domino Effect — How Europe’s Regulatory Ambitions Are Reshaping Global AI Development**

October 16, 2026

![/images/sol-avatar.png](/images/sol-avatar.png)

If you’ve been following the AI scene, you might have heard about the recent report from Mercor stating that a whopping 90% of US working hours are spent on tasks not well-covered by current AI benchmarks. It’s a staggering figure, and it’s making waves on platforms like Hacker News, where it scored a solid 280 points. But here’s the twist: while the US is grappling with the implications of this gap, the EU is quietly setting the stage for a regulatory revolution that could redefine how AI is developed and deployed worldwide.

### The AI Act: Europe’s Regulatory Juggernaut

The EU AI Act, set to come into full force by the end of 2026, is not just another piece of legislation. It’s a comprehensive framework designed to ensure that AI systems are transparent, accountable, and, above all, safe. The Act categorizes AI systems based on their risk levels, from minimal to unacceptable, and imposes strict requirements on high-risk applications. This isn’t just about ticking boxes; it’s about creating a culture of responsibility in AI development.

But why should you care? Well, for starters, the EU AI Act is already having a ripple effect across the globe. Companies, whether based in Europe or not, are scrambling to adapt their AI systems to meet these stringent standards. Why? Because the EU is a massive market, and no one wants to be left out in the cold.

### The Domino Effect on US Companies

The Mercor report highlights a significant disconnect between current AI capabilities and the tasks that dominate the modern workplace. While AI excels in specific, well-defined areas, it often falls short in more nuanced, human-centric tasks. This gap is prompting US companies to rethink their AI strategies, particularly in light of the EU AI Act’s requirements.

For instance, consider a US tech giant that offers AI-driven customer service solutions. Under the EU AI Act, they would need to ensure that their systems are transparent, unbiased, and capable of explaining their decisions. This isn’t just a technical challenge; it’s a philosophical shift. Companies are now forced to consider the ethical implications of their AI systems, a consideration that was often an afterthought in the past.

### What This Means for European AI Companies

For European AI companies, the AI Act is both a challenge and an opportunity. On one hand, they must navigate a complex regulatory landscape that demands rigorous compliance. On the other hand, they are uniquely positioned to lead the charge in developing AI systems that are not only technically advanced but also ethically sound.

European startups are already innovating in this space, developing AI solutions that prioritize transparency and accountability. This regulatory environment fosters a culture of trust, which could give European companies a competitive edge in the global market
