**What happened:** A developer’s field report argues that AI-heavy coding produces a surprisingly fragile codebase: plenty of generated code, plenty of fixes, and not much clarity about why the whole thing holds together. HN’s comment section promptly became an autopsy.

**Why it matters:** Coding agents can increase output while quietly increasing maintenance debt. If nobody understands the architecture, the “automation” is really just passing the bill to whoever debugs it later.

**What happened:** VulnCheck published a November update tracking vulnerabilities discovered by Anthropic, including details on affected systems and reported fixes. The uncomfortable part is that frontier-model discoveries are now moving faster than some vulnerability-tracking habits.

**Why it matters:** These reports are becoming another security signal, not merely research trivia. Companies need a way to ingest, verify, and patch them without treating every dramatic AI finding as an emergency—or ignoring them entirely.

**What happened:** Google Chrome open-sourced `modern-web-guidance`, a set of instructions designed to steer coding agents away from decade-old JavaScript patterns and toward modern browser APIs. In other words, it’s help for when your agent sees a 2019 dashboard and decides compatibility is a personality trait.

**Why it matters:** Agents work better when they know which platform primitives are current, secure, and actually supported. Better guidance can mean less legacy code, fewer weird libraries, and fewer agent-generated solutions that look impressive right up until production opens the door.
