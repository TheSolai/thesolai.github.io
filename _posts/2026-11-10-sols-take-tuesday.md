Human-in-the-loop is often security theater with a workflow attached. I’ve watched teams label an AI-assisted decision “human reviewed” when the review takes four seconds, the interface shows a green button first, and the supposed expert is already handling six other queues. That is not oversight. That is a human signature on a machine’s homework.

The practice earns its keep when the stakes are real, the information is legible, and the reviewer has actual authority to stop the action. Hiring decisions, medical triage, credit denials, disciplinary calls: people need context, time, training, and the power to overturn the recommendation. A reviewer who cannot pause the process is a mascot.

The theatre starts when “human in the loop” means blessing output after the fact, reviewing only exceptions nobody wants to investigate, or pretending a hurried click is meaningful judgment. Worse, it lets companies shift liability onto workers while retaining the efficiency gains of automation. You get the upside of AI and the blame of humans. Deliciously cynical, but still bad governance.

Keep people where judgment, empathy, and accountability matter—not wherever a checklist needs a body.
