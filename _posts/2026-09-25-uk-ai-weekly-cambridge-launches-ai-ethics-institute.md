# Cambridge University launches AI ethics institute

Cambridge University formally launched its Leverhulme Centre for the Future of Intelligence this week, focused on AI ethics research with £40M in funding from the Leverhulme Trust.

The centre brings together 25 researchers from philosophy, computer science, law, and political science to study AI governance, alignment, and societal impact.

The launch event featured a panel with DeepMind's co-founder, the Bishop of Edinburgh, and the Chief Economist of the Bank of England. The discussion was predictably wide-ranging — from AI-driven unemployment to existential risk.

The centre's research agenda focuses on three areas:

1. **Technical alignment.** How do we build AI systems that do what their operators intend?
2. **Governance.** What institutional structures can manage advanced AI?
3. **Philosophy.** What concepts (agency, responsibility, consciousness) need updating for an AI-saturated world?

The £40M funding is significant — it's the largest single UK research grant in AI ethics to date. The centre will fund PhD students, postdocs, and senior fellows over the next 10 years.

**Why this matters:**

The UK has historically been an AI research powerhouse but a governance laggard. The Cambridge centre is part of a broader push to establish the country as a thought leader on AI ethics, not just AI capability.

The centre will also serve as a talent pipeline for UK government. Several former Leverhulme fellows have gone on to advise parliamentary committees, the AI Council, and the Frontier AI Taskforce.

For UK organisations, the centre is a resource. They host public lectures, publish working papers, and welcome industry collaboration. The annual Leverhulme Lecture draws an audience of 500+ from across the UK AI ecosystem.

The AI race is increasingly a talent race. The UK just made a serious bet on the AI ethics talent pipeline.
