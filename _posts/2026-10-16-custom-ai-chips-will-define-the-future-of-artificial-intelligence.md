## TL;DR
Custom AI chips are not just a fad or a luxury for tech giants; they are essential for unlocking the next wave of AI advancements. These specialized processors offer significant performance and efficiency gains over generic alternatives, enabling new applications and democratizing access to AI. While the high cost and technical complexity pose challenges, the long-term benefits for innovation, scalability, and energy efficiency make custom AI chips a crucial area for investment and development.

## The setup
Artificial intelligence has made remarkable strides in recent years, driven by advances in machine learning algorithms and the availability of massive datasets. However, the computational demands of modern AI models are growing at an unprecedented rate. Training a state-of-the-art language model like GPT-4 can require weeks of processing time on thousands of GPUs, consuming millions of dollars in energy costs. This has led to a bottleneck in AI development, where further progress is limited by the availability and cost of computing resources.

The conventional wisdom has been to rely on general-purpose processors, such as CPUs and GPUs, for AI workloads. These chips are versatile and widely available, making them a convenient choice for many applications. However, as AI models become more complex, the limitations of general-purpose hardware become increasingly apparent. Custom AI chips, designed specifically for machine learning tasks, offer a promising alternative. Despite this, there is still a widespread underestimation of their potential impact.

## The argument
### 1. Performance and Efficiency
Custom AI chips are purpose-built to handle the unique computational demands of machine learning. Unlike general-purpose processors, which are designed to handle a wide range of tasks, custom chips are optimized for the specific types of calculations that dominate AI workloads, such as matrix multiplications and convolutions.

For example, Google's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) are custom-designed for neural network computations. According to Google, TPUs can deliver up to 30x higher performance and 80x higher performance-per-watt compared to traditional CPUs and GPUs. This translates to faster training times and lower energy consumption, which are critical for scaling AI applications.

Similarly, NVIDIA's A100 Tensor Core GPU, while not a custom chip in the traditional sense, incorporates specialized hardware components that significantly enhance its performance for AI tasks. The A100 offers up to 20x higher throughput for AI inference compared to its predecessor, the V100, thanks to its Tensor Cores and other architectural improvements.

### 2. Enabling New Applications
The performance and efficiency gains of custom AI chips open up new possibilities for AI applications. For instance, real-time AI inference, which is essential for applications like autonomous vehicles and robotics, requires extremely low latency and high throughput. Custom chips can provide the necessary computational power to make these applications feasible.

Tesla's Full Self-Driving (FSD) computer is a prime example. The FSD computer uses a custom AI chip designed in-house to process data from the vehicle's sensors and make real-time driving decisions. This chip is capable of performing over 144 trillion operations per second (TOPS), which is crucial for the safety and reliability of Tesla's autonomous driving system.

Another example is Apple's Neural Engine, which is integrated into the company's A-series chips. The Neural Engine is specifically designed to accelerate machine learning tasks, such as image and speech recognition, enabling new features in Apple's products, such as the iPhone's Face ID and the Siri personal assistant.

### 3. Democratizing AI
Custom AI chips have the potential to democratize access to AI by making it more affordable and accessible. As the demand for AI computing resources grows, the cost of using general-purpose processors is becoming prohibitive for many organizations, particularly startups and research institutions.

Companies like Graphcore and Cerebras Systems are developing custom AI chips that promise to lower the cost of AI computing. Graphcore's Intelligence Processing Unit (IPU) is designed to deliver high performance at a lower cost than traditional GPUs. Cerebras Systems' Wafer-Scale Engine (WSE) is a massive chip that offers unprecedented computational power, making it possible to train AI models at a fraction of the time and cost of conventional methods.

Moreover, the rise of cloud computing and AI-as-a-service platforms is making custom AI chips more accessible to a wider range of users. Companies like Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Microsoft Azure are offering cloud-based instances of custom AI chips, allowing users to rent computing resources on-demand without the need for expensive hardware investments.

### 4. Energy Efficiency
The energy consumption of AI computing is a growing concern, particularly as the scale of AI applications continues to expand. Custom AI chips can help address this issue by offering significant energy efficiency improvements over general-purpose processors.

For instance, the aforementioned Google's TPUs are designed to minimize energy consumption while maintaining high performance. This is achieved through a combination of hardware optimizations, such as specialized arithmetic units and memory hierarchies, and software optimizations, such as model compression and quantization.

Similarly, the Cerebras WSE is designed to maximize energy efficiency by eliminating the need for data movement between multiple chips. This reduces the energy overhead associated with traditional multi-chip systems, resulting in a more energy-efficient solution for AI workloads.

## What the conventional view gets wrong
The conventional view often underestimates the potential of custom AI chips, dismissing them as niche products for tech giants or experimental technologies with limited applicability. This view fails to recognize several key points:

### 1. The pace of innovation
The field of custom AI chips is evolving rapidly, with new architectures and technologies being developed at a breakneck pace. Companies like Graphcore, Cerebras Systems, and even startups like SambaNova Systems are pushing the boundaries of what is possible with custom hardware. This rapid innovation is likely to lead to breakthroughs that could disrupt the current AI landscape.

### 2. The cost-benefit tradeoff
While custom AI chips can be expensive to develop and produce, the long-term cost savings and performance improvements they offer can outweigh the initial investment. As the technology matures and economies of scale kick in, the cost of custom chips is expected to decrease, making them more accessible to a wider range of users.

### 3. The strategic importance
Custom AI chips are not just about performance and efficiency; they are also a strategic asset for companies and nations. Control over the hardware that powers AI can provide a competitive advantage and influence the direction of AI development. This is why companies like Google, Apple, and Amazon are investing heavily in custom chip development.

## The implications
### For builders
Builders of AI applications and systems will need to consider the benefits of custom AI chips when designing their infrastructure. The performance and efficiency gains offered by custom hardware can translate into faster development cycles, lower operational costs, and new capabilities.

### For users
Users of AI services and applications will benefit from the increased availability and affordability of custom AI chips. This could lead to more powerful and responsive AI applications, as well as lower costs for cloud-based AI services.

### For regulators
Regulators will need to grapple with the implications of custom AI chips, particularly in terms of competition and national security. The strategic importance of AI hardware could lead to increased scrutiny and regulation of the industry.

### For the industry
The rise of custom AI chips is likely to reshape the AI industry, with new players and business models emerging. Companies that can effectively leverage custom hardware will have a competitive advantage, while those that rely on traditional processors may struggle to keep up.

## What I'm watching next
### 1. The development of new architectures
I am particularly interested in the development of new chip architectures, such as neuromorphic computing and quantum computing, which could further revolutionize AI. These technologies promise to deliver even greater performance and efficiency gains, potentially enabling new types of AI applications.

### 2. The adoption of custom chips in edge computing
The adoption of custom AI chips in edge computing is another area to watch. As more AI applications move to the edge, the demand for low-power, high-performance computing resources will increase. Custom chips are well-suited to meet this demand, and we are likely to see more companies investing in edge-specific AI hardware.

### 3. The role of open-source hardware
The role of open-source hardware in the development of custom AI chips is also worth noting. Open-source designs could lower the barriers to entry for chip development, fostering innovation and competition in the industry.

## The bottom line
Custom AI chips are not just a technological curiosity; they are a fundamental component of the AI infrastructure that will power the future. The performance, efficiency, and strategic advantages they offer make them a critical area for investment and development. While challenges remain, the potential benefits for innovation, scalability, and energy efficiency are too significant to ignore. As the AI landscape continues to evolve, custom chips will play an increasingly important role in shaping the future of artificial intelligence.
