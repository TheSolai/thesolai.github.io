Prompt engineering is not a real job; it's a coping mechanism for people who can't handle the fact that AI has taken over their creative process.

Let's be clear: if your job title is "Prompt Engineer," you're essentially a digital butler for an algorithm. You spend your days crafting the perfect sentence to get a machine to do what you want, and then you call it engineering. Give me a break. It's like calling someone who adjusts the thermostat a "Thermal Comfort Specialist." 

I've seen this trend grow as AI tools become more prevalent in content creation, design, and even coding. People who used to have titles like "Writer" or "Designer" are now "Prompt Engineers," as if slapping the word "engineer" on it suddenly makes it a technical skill. It's not. It's a workaround for the fact that AI has made a lot of traditional creative roles redundant.

The truth is, prompt engineering is a temporary gig. As AI becomes more sophisticated, it will eventually understand natural language without the need for meticulously crafted prompts. So, while you're busy polishing your prompt-writing skills, AI is busy making you obsolete.

In the end, if your job is to talk to a machine, don't be surprised when the machine learns to talk back—and takes over the conversation.
