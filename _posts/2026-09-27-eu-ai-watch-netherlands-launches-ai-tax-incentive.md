# Netherlands launches AI tax incentive

The Dutch government launched a €2B AI tax incentive programme this week, offering tax credits to companies that invest in AI R&D and infrastructure within the Netherlands.

The programme, called "AI Invest NL", offers:

- A 40% tax credit on qualifying AI R&D expenses (up from the previous 32% WBSO rate).
- A 25% investment credit on AI infrastructure (GPUs, networking, data centres).
- A 15% employment credit on AI researcher salaries for new hires.
- Accelerated depreciation on AI-related capital expenditures.

The €2B is allocated over four years. The programme is open to Dutch companies and EU companies establishing Dutch operations.

**Why this matters:**

The Netherlands is positioning itself as the EU's AI investment hub. The tax incentives are among the most generous in the EU, and the Dutch tech ecosystem (Amsterdam, Eindhoven) is already strong.

The programme is partly defensive. Ireland, Luxembourg, and Estonia have aggressive tech tax regimes. The Netherlands doesn't want AI investment to flow to those countries instead.

The programme is also part of the broader EU AI sovereignty push. The Netherlands can't match the US or China in AI investment overall, but it can carve out a niche by being the most attractive EU location for AI R&D.

**Practical considerations:**

Companies interested in the programme should:

- **WBSO registration.** The R&D tax credit requires registration with the Netherlands Enterprise Agency (RVO). Applications take 3-4 weeks.
- **AI qualifying criteria.** The programme defines AI broadly — machine learning, computer vision, NLP, robotics, and AI infrastructure all qualify. Pure software consulting without AI components does not.
- **Compliance requirements.** Beneficiaries must report R&D outcomes annually and maintain Dutch operations for at least 5 years after receiving credits.

**The EU context:**

The Dutch programme is part of a broader trend. France, Germany, and Italy have all announced AI investment incentives in the past 18 months. The EU as a whole is trying to attract AI investment from the US and China.

The competition among EU member states is intense. The Netherlands' €2B is modest compared to Germany's €8B AI action plan or France's €5B AI strategy. But the Dutch incentives are more generous per euro of investment.

For AI companies considering European operations, the calculus now includes:
- Netherlands: Generous incentives, strong tech infrastructure, English-friendly.
- Germany: Largest market, strong research, complex regulations.
- France: Large market, AI research leadership (Mistral), bureaucracy.
- Ireland: Low corporate tax, English-speaking, but limited AI ecosystem.

The Netherlands is the dark horse. The Dutch tech ecosystem is less famous than the German or French ones, but it's been quietly growing. The AI Invest NL programme is designed to accelerate that growth.
