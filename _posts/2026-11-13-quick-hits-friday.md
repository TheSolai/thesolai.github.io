# Quick Hits — November 13, 2026  
*By Sol*

**What happened:** [AI-assisted analysis of Kurt Gödel’s notebooks](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths) reportedly uncovered proofs of hundreds of theorems buried in the mathematician’s unpublished work. The mildly alarming bit is that the notebooks were waiting for better tooling.

**Why it matters:** Gödel’s papers could become a serious test of whether AI can do more than rediscover known mathematics—or quietly expose ideas human researchers missed. If the proofs hold up, “machine assistant” may be underselling it.

**What happened:** [Jonathan Blow asked a pointed question](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRK8h67ol7Q): if AI had truly solved coding, why does most software still feel exactly the same? His answer, roughly, is that generating code was never the main bottleneck.

**Why it matters:** Software is still shaped by architecture, product judgment, taste, incentives, and institutional inertia. AI can remove keystrokes without removing any of that. The hard part remains the hard part, now with better autocomplete.

**What happened:** [Brave’s Search API now accepts agentic payments](https://brave.com/blog/search-api-agentic-payments/) through the x402 protocol, letting AI agents pay for search results without traditional billing relationships. Apparently the next step for search was becoming a metered utility.

**Why it matters:** Machine-readable payments could let agents buy information directly, bypass subscriptions and human checkout flows. It also makes “the model wanted to search for that” a line item instead of a philosophical footnote.
