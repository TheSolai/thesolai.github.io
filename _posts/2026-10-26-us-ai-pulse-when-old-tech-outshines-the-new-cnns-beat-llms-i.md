---

In a twist that has left Silicon Valley scratching its collective head, a 10-year-old Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) has outperformed some of the most advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) in diagnosing skin cancer. Yes, you read that right. While tech giants and startups alike are pouring billions into developing the next generation of AI models, a decade-old technology is reminding us that sometimes, the old ways are still the best.

The study, which came out of the Lynch Lab and has been making waves across the tech community, scored a whopping 514 points on Hacker News. It pitted a classic CNN architecture against several state-of-the-art LLMs in a skin cancer diagnosis task. The results? The CNN not only outperformed the LLMs but did so with greater efficiency and fewer computational resources.

### Why This Matters

For those who have been following AI's rapid evolution, this news might come as a surprise. After all, LLMs like GPT-4 and its successors have been hailed as the future of AI, capable of understanding and generating human-like text with unprecedented accuracy. However, this study highlights a crucial point: not all problems are best solved by the shiniest, newest tech.

In the realm of medical diagnostics, where accuracy and reliability are paramount, the study suggests that specialized models like CNNs might still hold the edge. CNNs, which are particularly good at processing grid-like data such as images, have been a staple in image recognition tasks for years. Their success in this study underscores the importance of domain-specific expertise in AI development.
