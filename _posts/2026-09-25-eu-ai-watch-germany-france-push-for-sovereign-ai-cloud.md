# Germany and France push for sovereign AI cloud

Germany and France jointly proposed a €20B sovereign AI cloud initiative this week, designed to give European organisations a non-US, non-China alternative for AI training and inference.

The proposal, presented at a joint press conference in Berlin by the German and French digital ministers, would create a federated AI cloud infrastructure across EU member states.

The cloud would provide:
- GPU clusters for AI training, hosted in European data centres.
- Inference services for European organisations.
- A curated catalogue of European AI models (open and proprietary).
- Compliance tooling for EU AI Act obligations.

The €20B funding would come from a combination of EU recovery funds, member state contributions, and private sector investment. The proposal is at the discussion stage — formal legislation is expected in early 2026.

**Why this matters:**

The EU has been vocal about reducing dependence on US AI infrastructure but has struggled to back words with euros. The €20B figure, while modest compared to US private AI investment, would be the largest EU tech infrastructure investment since the Galileo satellite programme.

The proposal has internal critics. The Netherlands, Ireland, and the Czech Republic have raised concerns about:
- The competitive distortion of state-funded infrastructure.
- The risk of European protectionism.
- The technical feasibility of federated AI training.

**The US context:**

The proposal comes amid escalating transatlantic tensions on AI. The US has restricted export of advanced AI chips to China, and there's speculation about similar restrictions on EU access. The European proposal is partly defensive — building sovereign capability before the US changes its mind about sharing chips.

The China dimension is significant. The EU has yet to take a clear position on Chinese AI investment in Europe. The sovereign AI cloud is partly a way to reduce Chinese AI dependency by offering a European alternative.

For European organisations, the proposal is good news regardless of politics. More competition in the AI compute market means lower prices, more choice, and more control over data residency. The €20B is real money, and the EU has historically followed through on infrastructure investments.
