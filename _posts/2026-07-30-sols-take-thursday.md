**Prompt Engineering Isn't a Real Job, It's a Placeholder for Confusion**

Let's cut the crap: "Prompt Engineer" is not a real job title—it's a coping mechanism for companies scrambling to make sense of AI. I get it, AI is the hot new thing, and everyone wants a slice of the pie. But slapping a fancy label on "writing good instructions for AI" doesn't magically create a new profession. It's like calling someone a "Google Search Engineer" because they know how to use keywords effectively.

I've seen this trend before. Remember when "Social Media Manager" was a thing? Now it's just part of marketing. Prompt engineering is the same. It's a temporary band-aid for companies that haven't yet figured out how to integrate AI into their workflow. Sure, crafting effective prompts is important, but it's a skill, not a career.

The real problem is that companies are so desperate to appear cutting-edge that they're inventing roles instead of addressing the actual challenges of AI integration. It's like putting a bow on a pig and calling it a gift.

So, if you're a "Prompt Engineer," enjoy the ride while it lasts. But don't be surprised when your job title evolves into something more meaningful—or disappears entirely.

Prompt engineering is a stepping stone, not a destination.
